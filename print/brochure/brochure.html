A note from the kitchen table

Mom does the stitching.
We do the typing.

Lusik came to Los Angeles from Armenia in the late 1970s with a cross-stitch hoop and the way of working her mother and grandmother had taught her. She lived in East Hollywood, the neighborhood people call Little Armenia, and later moved south to Orange County, where she stitches today.

One letter, one small X at a time.

Every piece in this brochure comes off her kitchen table in Buena Park. She works on one order at a time and checks each piece herself before it goes in the box.

Her sons built the website and put this brochure together. If you have a question, one of us will answer it. If it is a question about the stitching, we will go and ask her.

The blankets

The first letters a child learns.

The Armenian Alphabet Blanket

Ա Բ Գ in Armenian, or A B C in English. Lusik cross-stitches the three letters twice, along two diagonals that cross the blanket corner to corner, onto soft cloth woven with a small pomegranate pattern. Satin backing, fringed edges, 52 by 52 inches. Add a name or a year if you like.

Allow 4 to 6 weeksColors vary

The Full Alphabet Crib Blanket

Every letter of the Armenian alphabet, hand-knit into a crib blanket and finished with a crochet edge and a satin backing matched to the body. Blue, pink, lavender, mint, yellow, or dusty rose. Pick a color, not a shade: the yarn changes from one blanket to the next, so yours will not match the photo.

About the colors

Please read this before you order. Lusik works from the yarn and thread she has on hand, and dye lots change from one batch to the next. Your piece will be close to the photos here and online, never an exact match, and no two are quite the same. That goes for everything we make, and most of all for the Full Alphabet Crib Blanket, where the whole body is yarn. If a particular shade matters, call or message us first and we will tell you what she has.

Care

Dry cleaning is best for every piece. At home: cold water, delicate cycle, no bleach, no iron. Bibs may tumble dry low; lay the Armenian Alphabet Blanket flat to dry.
